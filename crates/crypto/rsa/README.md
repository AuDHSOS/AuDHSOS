# crypto-rsa

RSA signature verification, which is the one omission that costs this
client real chains: `www.ietf.org`, `www.rust-lang.org`, and
`www.bbc.co.uk` are all RSA to the root.

Two encodings, and they are not symmetric in how they are treated.
PKCS #1 v1.5 is verified by building the encoded message and comparing it
against the one the signature recovers — never by parsing what arrived
(D-80). RFC 8017, section 8.2.2 offers both shapes and the note after it
offers a decoder; a decoder is where a signature with slack in its
padding is accepted, and a construction has no slack to accept. The cost
is stated rather than discovered: a `DigestInfo` that is BER but not DER
is refused here and valid under PKCS #1 v1.5, whose section 10.2.3 makes
the BER decode its verification operation. That is a deliberate
incompatibility with a published specification.

PSS is EMSA-PSS-VERIFY as RFC 8017, section 9.1.2 writes it, in the
`rsae` shape and no other (D-81): MGF1 over the same hash that made the
digest, and a salt as long as that hash's output, which is what RFC 8446
fixes for the three schemes this client offers.

What the key rules are, and where they are not. `PublicKey::new` takes
the shape rules that belong to the primitive: the modulus odd with its
top bit set and no wider than the arithmetic allows, the exponent odd and
at least three. It does not take the lower bound on the key size. That
rule belongs to whoever judges a certificate and lives in
`audhsos-x509` (D-79) — putting it here would make the one real RSA
vector this repository holds, the thousand-and-twenty-four-bit key of
RFC 8448, section 2, unusable by the crate that ought to check itself
against it.

Signing exists only behind the feature `test-signing`, and it is one call
into the exponentiation verification already has. No key is generated by
project code (D-83).

Nothing here is constant time, and `crypto-bignum` says why: a modulus, an
exponent, and a signature are all on the wire, and no secret enters
either crate. The comparison of the encoded messages still goes through
`ct_eq`, because a comparison with no early exit is the right habit here
whether or not this call needs it.
